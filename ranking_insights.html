How it was made

Real survey respondents (147,555 of them) were shown pairs of characters from different franchises and asked to rate how similar they felt — from "Extremely Similar" to "Extremely Different." These are the pairs most consistently rated as similar, with at least 20 different raters agreeing.

What you're looking at

Each card shows two character portraits side-by-side. The percentage in the middle is the average similarity score (0–100), where 100 = "Extremely Similar." The number below shows how many survey respondents rated that pair.

How to interpret it

High scores mean many people independently felt these two characters "feel the same" — even across completely different shows. This human judgment captures things personality scores might miss: visual appearance, role archetype, gender, tone of storytelling, and cultural resonance.

Top 30 Most Similar Pairs

How it was made

For each character pair, all individual similarity ratings were averaged into a single score. Only pairs with at least 20 ratings are shown, to ensure the average is reliable and not skewed by one or two unusual responses.

What you're looking at

A horizontal bar chart, sorted from most to least similar. Blue bars = same-franchise pairs. Red bars = cross-franchise pairs (characters from different shows). The x-axis is a 0–100 similarity score.

How to interpret it

A score near 80–100 means raters strongly agreed these two characters are alike. A score near 40–50 means raters were split between "slightly similar" and "slightly different." Same-franchise pairs at the top might mean very similar character archetypes were written into one show on purpose.

Do Personality Scores Predict Human Perception?

Each dot = one character pair. We'd expect: closer BAP scores → higher human similarity rating.

How it was made

For 2,000 character pairs, we computed two things: (1) their personality distance using the 500 BAP trait scores — smaller = more similar personalities — and (2) how similar real humans rated them in the survey. If personality scores perfectly predicted human perception, every dot would follow a downward-sloping line.

What you're looking at

Each dot is one character pair. The x-axis is their computed personality distance (left = very similar scores, right = very different scores). The y-axis is how similar humans rated them (top = very similar, bottom = very different). The r value in the corner is the Pearson correlation coefficient.

How to interpret it

An r of 0.10 is a weak positive correlation — personality scores do predict human similarity a little, but not much. This makes sense: humans judge similarity based on many things beyond personality (looks, role, franchise tone, cultural references). The scatter also tells you that even two characters with very similar BAP scores might feel totally different to a viewer, and vice versa.

Most Surprising Cross-Franchise Similarities

Characters from different shows/movies that people rated as highly similar

How it was made

Same method as the Top 30 chart, but filtered to only include pairs where the two characters come from completely different shows or movies. These are the most striking findings — characters from unrelated universes that people still perceived as essentially the same person.

What you're looking at

Each bar is a cross-franchise pair, labeled with both character names and their source franchises. The bar length shows the average human similarity score. All bars are red to distinguish them from same-franchise pairs.

How to interpret it

These are "personality twins" across universes — characters so fundamentally similar in vibe that audiences recognize the resemblance without being told. High scores here suggest writers across different franchises independently created the same archetypal character, which is itself a fascinating finding about storytelling patterns.
